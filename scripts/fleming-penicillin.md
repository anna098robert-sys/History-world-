# The Worst Cleaner in London

**Channel:** History World
**Format:** First-person storytelling, cartoon doodle
**Runtime:** ~2:40 voiceover (~440 words)
**Historical moment:** Alexander Fleming discovers penicillin. St Mary's Hospital, London, September 1928.

---

**[0:00, HOOK]**

I once saved millions of lives. By being the worst cleaner in London. My mom still tells me to clean my room. Mom, I have a very good reason not to.

> **SCENE:** The doodle guy stands in his messy bedroom, surrounded by pizza boxes and socks. He's holding a broom upside down and smiling proudly at the camera. His mom's arm points in angrily from off-screen. Record scratch, freeze frame.

---

**[0:10]**

So here's what happened. I was taking a nap in a library. Wake up, and I'm in London. 1928. Everything smells like rain and tea. A man in a white coat walks past, sees my face, and goes, "Ah, you must be the new lab assistant." I say, "Absolutely." I wasn't.

> **SCENE:** He blinks awake on a cobblestone street, rubbing his eyes. Foggy London behind him: red brick buildings, a double-decker bus, a "St Mary's Hospital" sign. A thin man in a white lab coat and bow tie looks him over. He gives a big confident thumbs-up, with a tiny sweat drop on his forehead.

---

**[0:28]**

The man is Alexander Fleming. A scientist. He studies bacteria. Tiny, invisible bugs that make people sick. He grows them in little glass dishes. Hundreds of them. And he's about to go on vacation.

> **SCENE:** A cramped, cluttered lab. Stacks of round glass petri dishes everywhere, microscopes, bottles and papers. Fleming packs a suitcase. The doodle guy pokes at a petri dish, fascinated, like a kid at an aquarium.

---

**[0:42]**

Before he leaves, he says, "Keep the lab tidy." I say, "You can count on me!" And then I do absolutely nothing for two weeks. I eat sandwiches. I open the window because it's stuffy. I build a tower out of petri dishes. It was a great vacation. For me.

> **SCENE:** A quick montage. He waves goodbye at the door with a huge grin. He eats a sandwich at Fleming's desk, crumbs falling onto the dishes. He opens a window and a breeze blows in. He stacks petri dishes into a wobbly tower, tongue out in concentration. Calendar pages flip by.

---

**[1:00]**

Then I hear footsteps. He's back. Early. I look at the lab. Fuzzy green stuff is growing on one of the dishes. Mold. I panic. I grab the dish and run to the sink. Hot water. Soap. Destroy the evidence.

> **SCENE:** He freezes, eyes wide, as a shadow appears behind the door's frosted glass. Close-up on a petri dish with a fuzzy blue-green blob. He snatches it and sprints to a sink full of soapy bubbles, sweating buckets, looking guilty.

---

**[1:15, TWIST]**

The dish is one inch from the water. And Fleming screams, "STOP!" I freeze. This is it. I'm fired. Maybe arrested. Maybe both. He grabs the dish, holds it up to the light, and goes very, very quiet. Then he whispers, "That's funny." Around the mold, all the bacteria are dead. Gone. The mold killed them.

> **SCENE:** Slow motion: the dish hovers over the bubbles while Fleming lunges in with an outstretched hand. The doodle guy is frozen mid-motion with a terrified face. Then Fleming holds the dish up to the window light in awe. Zoom in on the dish: a clear ring around the mold where the bacteria have vanished.

---

**[1:38]**

I said, "So... I'm not fired?" He said, "You might have just changed medicine forever." Turns out that mold makes something that kills germs. He called it penicillin. The first real antibiotic. It's saved millions and millions of lives since.

> **SCENE:** He peeks out from behind a lab stool, still scared. Fleming laughs and shakes his hand with both hands. The background melts into a sunny montage: old hospital wards, doctors, a pill bottle, happy healthy people. Heroic music.

---

**[1:58]**

Seventeen years later, Fleming wins the Nobel Prize. Big ceremony. Gold medal. Fancy speeches. And me? I'm not in a single history book. Not one. Not even a footnote. But I know what I did. I did nothing. Perfectly.

> **SCENE:** A grand hall with chandeliers and a crowd in tuxedos applauding Fleming on stage with a medal. In the very back row, the doodle guy sits alone in his red cap, clapping slowly with a proud, slightly tearful little smile. A single spotlight lands on him, then flickers off.

So here's the lesson. Sometimes a mess isn't a mistake. It's a discovery nobody's noticed yet. And Mom, if you're watching, my room isn't dirty. It's a laboratory. ### Production notes
- **Real facts in the script:** Alexander Fleming worked at St Mary's Hospital in London. He came back from a summer holiday in September 1928 and found a mold (Penicillium) on a culture dish that had killed the Staphylococcus bacteria around it. He called the substance penicillin. Fleming, Florey and Chain shared the Nobel Prize in 1945. His real reaction is often quoted as "That's funny."
- **Fiction:** The doodle guy, the sandwich, the open window and the sink rescue. How the mold got into the dish is still debated; one common theory is spores drifting up from a mycology lab on the floor below. It took Howard Florey and Ernst Chain in the late 1930s and 1940s to turn penicillin into a usable drug, so "changed medicine forever" is a comic shortcut.
