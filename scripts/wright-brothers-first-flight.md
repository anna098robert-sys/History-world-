# I Almost Ruined the First Airplane Flight

**Channel:** History World
**Format:** First-person storytelling, cartoon doodle
**Runtime:** ~2:30 voiceover (~400 words)
**Historical moment:** The Wright brothers' first powered flight. Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, December 17, 1903.

---

**[0:00, HOOK]**

The most famous photo in aviation history exists because a baseball cap hit a guy in the face. That was my cap. And that was the worst day of my life. Or the best one. I still can't decide.

> **SCENE:** Close-up on the doodle guy staring straight into the camera with a guilty half-smile, holding his red cap to his chest. Behind him the famous black-and-white photo of the first flight sits in a museum frame. Cut to black with a record-scratch sound.

---

**[0:12]**

So here's what happened. I was at an aviation museum. Big wooden airplane. Velvet rope. A sign that said "Do not touch." I touched it. Obviously. Everything goes white. And then I'm standing on a beach. In December. In a T-shirt.

> **SCENE:** The doodle guy, wide-eyed and curious, pokes the old wooden plane's wing with one finger. Flash of white light. Smash cut: he's on a grey, windswept sand dune, shivering, arms wrapped around himself, teeth chattering. A sign stuck in the sand reads "Kitty Hawk, 1903."

---

**[0:30]**

Two serious guys in suits are wheeling out a giant wooden thing. It looks like a kite that went to college. I walk up and say, "Cool kite, guys!" They look at me like I just insulted their mom. Turns out they're the Wright brothers. Orville and Wilbur. And that "kite" is the first airplane ever.

> **SCENE:** The doodle guy waves cheerfully and gives a thumbs-up. Two stiff, mustached figures in suits and caps glare back at him beside the Wright Flyer, a huge fabric-and-wood biplane on a wooden rail. Behind them, grey sky, sand dunes and a small wooden shed.

---

**[0:48]**

Nearby there's a local guy named John. Orville hands him a camera. John says, "I've never used one of these." I say, "Me neither!" Nobody asked me. Orville tells him, "When it lifts off, squeeze the bulb." Easy. What could go wrong?

> **SCENE:** A friendly man in a heavy coat nervously holds an old box camera on a tripod, rubber bulb in hand. The doodle guy leans over his shoulder, nodding like he's an expert. He's clearly confident and clearly clueless. Wind whips sand across the frame.

---

**[1:05, TWIST]**

The engine roars. The plane starts sliding down the rail. And then a gust of wind rips the cap right off my head. It flies across the beach and slaps John right in the face. Thwack! He flinches, and I hear a little click. My heart stops. I just ruined the first flight in human history. With a hat.

> **SCENE:** Slow motion. The red cap spins through the air like a frisbee. The doodle guy reaches after it with a horrified "NOOO" face. The cap smacks John's face and his hand clenches the camera bulb. Freeze frame on the doodle guy, frozen in pure panic, with a single sweat drop.

---

**[1:25]**

I run over screaming, "I'm so sorry! Did you miss it?!" And John just looks at the plane. It's in the air. Twelve seconds. A hundred and twenty feet. Orville's flying it, Wilbur's running next to it, and John goes, "I think I got it."

> **SCENE:** The doodle guy sprints toward John, arms flailing in total panic. In the background the Wright Flyer hovers just above the sand, with a tiny Wilbur running beside the wingtip. The doodle guy slowly turns his head, mouth open, eyes huge.

---

**[1:45]**

He did get it. The flinch was perfect. Right moment, right angle. One of the most famous photos ever taken. And I'm the reason it got taken. Me. And my hat.

> **SCENE:** The image freezes and drains into the iconic black-and-white photo. Pull back to reveal the doodle guy hugging his red cap like a newborn, tears of joy streaming down his face. Soft heroic music swells.

---

**[1:58]**

Then everything goes white again, and I'm back in the museum. A security guard is giving me a look. I check the famous photo on the wall. And there in the corner, in the sand, is a tiny little red dot. Nobody's ever noticed it. Except me.

> **SCENE:** Back in the bright museum. A stern guard glares with crossed arms. The doodle guy tiptoes up to the framed photo, squints, and the camera zooms into the corner: a tiny red cap half-buried in the sand. He turns to the viewer and gives a slow, smug wink.

---

**[2:15, MORAL + CTA]**

So here's the lesson. You don't have to be a genius to make history. Sometimes you just have to be in the wrong place, at the right time, with a really loose hat. And if you want to find out what other moments I accidentally messed up, subscribe to History World. Hit the bell. And hold on to your hat!

> **SCENE:** The doodle guy stands in front of the museum's airplane, puts his red cap back on and pulls it down tight with both hands. A gust of wind blows anyway. He grabs it, laughs, and points at a big animated "SUBSCRIBE" button that pops up beside him, with a bouncing bell icon. End card: the History World logo.

### Production notes
- **Real facts in the script:** December 17, 1903, Kitty Hawk, NC. Orville piloted the first flight while Wilbur ran alongside. It lasted 12 seconds and covered 120 feet. The photo was taken by John T. Daniels, a local lifesaving-station crewman who had never used a camera before.
- **Fiction:** The cap, the flinch and the doodle guy. The real photo was taken by John Daniels, as Orville had arranged.
