## Fact check

- **Pisa:** construction began in 1173; the tilt appeared after the third floor (c. 1178) because of soft clay, sand and shell deposits. Wars stopped work for almost a century (resumed 1272), which let the soil consolidate. The bell chamber was finished in 1372. Stabilisation work in 1990-2001 brought the lean to about 4°.
- **Newton:** Cambridge closed during the Great Plague of 1665-66, and Newton worked at Woolsthorpe Manor. He told the falling-apple story himself late in life (the apple hitting his head is a later myth). *Principia* was published in 1687.
- **Fleming:** in summer 1928 Fleming left staphylococcus cultures on his bench at St Mary's Hospital, London, and went on holiday. He came back on 3 September 1928 and found a *Penicillium* mould killing the bacteria around it. The spores probably came from the mycology lab one floor below. Florey and Chain turned penicillin into a usable drug; all three shared the 1945 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
- The red cap, the doodle guy and every 'accident' he causes are fiction, played for laughs.
