## Fact check

- **Voyage:** the Titanic sailed from Southampton on 10 April 1912 (calling at Cherbourg and Queenstown), bound for New York. Newspapers and promotional material described it as *practically unsinkable*.
- **Size and luxury:** about 269 m long, four funnels (the fourth was mostly for ventilation), a Grand Staircase under a glass dome, a gymnasium, a swimming pool, a squash court and multi-course first-class menus.
- **Lifeboats:** 20 lifeboats with room for about 1,178 people, for roughly 2,224 people on board. Many early boats were lowered partly empty (lifeboat 7, the first launched, carried about 28 people with room for 65).
- **Captain:** Edward John Smith commanded the ship.
- **Binoculars:** the crow's-nest lookouts had no binoculars; the key to the locker was kept by officer David Blair, who was removed from the crew before the voyage.
- **The iceberg:** lookout Frederick Fleet rang the bell three times and phoned the bridge ("Iceberg, right ahead!") at about 11:40 pm on 14 April. The sea was unusually calm and there was no moon. Some passengers played with ice that fell on the deck.
- **The sinking:** the ship sank at about 2:20 am on 15 April 1912. More than 1,500 people died; RMS Carpathia rescued about 705 survivors from the lifeboats. The ship's band played to keep passengers calm.
- **Afterwards:** the first International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS, 1914) required enough lifeboat places for everyone on board.
- **Film history joke:** synchronised-sound feature films ("talkies") arrived in 1927, so in 1912 a "movie" would have been a silent film.
- The hero, his warnings, the napkin iceberg and his time machine are fiction. The tragedy is shown respectfully: no deaths are depicted, and the humour stays on the fictional character.
