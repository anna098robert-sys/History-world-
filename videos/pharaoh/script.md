## Fact check

- **White pyramids:** the Giza pyramids were faced with polished white Tura limestone. Most of the casing was stripped in the Middle Ages and reused in Cairo, exposing the stepped core.
- **Red crown:** the Deshret was the red crown of Lower Egypt; the Pharaoh's crook and flail were symbols of kingship. The misunderstanding is fiction.
- **Cats:** cats were deeply respected and linked to the goddess Bastet; many were mummified.
- **Pyramid builders:** excavations of the workers' village and cemeteries at Giza (Mark Lehner, Zahi Hawass) show organised, fed and medically cared-for labour crews, not slaves.
- **The inundation:** the Nile flooded every summer, depositing fertile black silt (the Egyptians called their land Kemet, 'the black land'). Unusually high floods could destroy villages and stores.
- **Irrigation:** Egyptians managed floodwater with basins, dykes and canals; the shaduf, a counterweighted lever for lifting water, appears in New Kingdom tomb paintings.
- **Tomb paintings:** scenes of farming and irrigation are common in tombs at Thebes (e.g. the tomb of Ipuy, c. 1250 BC). The painting in the book is invented, and so is the red cap in it.
- The slide, the chariot crash and the hero's reign are fiction, played for laughs.
