## Fact check

- **Date and place:** Napoleon was crowned Emperor of the French at Notre-Dame de Paris on 2 December 1804 (11 Frimaire, Year XIII). Pope Pius VII travelled from Rome to attend.
- **The Pope waiting:** Pius VII left the Tuileries before Napoleon and waited in the cold cathedral for about an hour until the imperial procession arrived. The real delay came from the ceremony's schedule, not from a lost coachman.
- **Goldsmith and wreath:** the golden laurel wreath was made by the goldsmith Martin-Guillaume Biennais. It proved too heavy, so six leaves were removed; one was sold by the auction house Osenat at Fontainebleau in 2017 for €625,000 (with fees).
- **The coach:** the coronation coach was gilded and pulled by eight horses.
- **The cathedral:** after the Revolution Notre-Dame was in poor condition. For the ceremony it was dressed with temporary decorations, painted panels and a neo-Gothic porch designed by the architects Percier and Fontaine.
- **The train bearers:** Napoleon's sisters carried Josephine's train reluctantly. Accounts say they dropped or tugged it at one point, so that Josephine was briefly held back.
- **Self-coronation:** Napoleon took the crown from the altar and placed it on his own head, a step planned in advance with the Pope. He then crowned Josephine himself.
- **Height:** Napoleon's height was recorded as 5 pieds 2 pouces in French units, about 1.69 m. That was average or slightly above average for a Frenchman of his time. British caricaturists such as James Gillray popularised 'Little Boney'.
- **David's painting:** Jacques-Louis David painted *The Coronation of Napoleon* in 1805-1807 (Louvre, about 6.2 x 9.8 m). He added Napoleon's mother (Madame Mère, Letizia Bonaparte) in the central gallery, although she did not attend; she had stayed in Rome.
- The hero, the shortcut, the sneeze, the flying crown and the two leaves in his pocket are fiction, played for laughs.
