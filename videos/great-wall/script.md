## Fact check

- **Many walls:** walls were built and rebuilt by many states and dynasties from the 7th century BC onward. Qin Shi Huang linked earlier walls after 221 BC; the Ming (1368-1644) built most of what tourists see today. China's 2012 national survey measured all wall sections at 21,196 km.
- **Jinshanling and Qi Jiguang:** the Jinshanling section was built from about 1570 under General Qi Jiguang (戚继光), who introduced two-storey hollow watchtowers where soldiers could live and store weapons.
- **Builders:** soldiers, conscripted peasants and convicts all worked on the wall.
- **Stamped bricks:** many Ming bricks carry inscriptions naming the year, the kiln or unit, or the officials responsible, so defects could be traced. 隆慶四年 = the 4th year of the Longqing reign = 1570 (written in traditional characters, as in the period).
- **Sticky-rice mortar:** Ming builders mixed sticky-rice soup with slaked lime. A 2010 study (Zhejiang University) found that amylopectin from the rice makes the mortar stronger and more water resistant.
- **Beacon code:** under a 1466 Ming regulation, one smoke/fire and one cannon shot signalled about 100 raiders, two about 500, three 1,000 or more (four and five meant larger armies). Smoke was used by day, fire by night.
- **1571 peace:** the Longqing Peace with the Mongol leader Altan Khan opened border horse markets and brought decades of relative calm.
- **Visible from space:** a myth. Yang Liwei, China's first astronaut (Shenzhou 5, October 2003), said he could not see the wall with the naked eye.
- The hero, his campfire, the double-rice section and the raid he 'stops' are fiction, played for laughs.
