## Fact check

- **The theft:** the Mona Lisa was stolen from the Salon Carré of the Louvre on Monday 21 August 1911, a day the museum was closed to the public. Only the four iron pegs remained on the wall; the loss was noticed the next day.
- **The thief:** Vincenzo Peruggia, an Italian who had worked at the Louvre, wore a white workman's smock like the staff, carried the painting out under it, and kept it in a trunk in his Paris room for over two years. He was caught in Florence in December 1913 when he tried to sell it to the Uffizi.
- **Fame:** crowds came to the Louvre to stare at the empty space, and the worldwide coverage helped make it the most famous painting in the world.
- **The painting:** oil on a poplar wood panel, about 77 × 53 cm.
- **The Louvre:** a former royal palace; it opened as a public museum in 1793. The glass pyramid did not exist until 1989, so it is not shown. The Winged Victory of Samothrace has stood at the top of the Daru staircase since 1883.
- **Paris 1911:** horse-drawn fiacres, gendarmes in képis, gas street lamps and the Eiffel Tower (1889, 22 years old in 1911).
- **Fiction:** the hero, the chase, the early return of the painting and the red-cap hero in the history books are invented for comedy.
